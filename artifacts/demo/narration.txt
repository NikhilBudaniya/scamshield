An invoice can look familiar while its payment destination has changed. ScamShield gives a person a review step before an invoice reaches a simulated assessment. The goal is to compare invoice details with a known vendor record and keep the evidence behind the recommendation visible. This walkthrough uses synthetic invoices on the public demo, including invented vendor names, account identifiers and amounts. The interface keeps the person in control of the next step. The app never sends a payment.

I’ll upload a synthetic invoice. Vertex AI Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite returns candidate fields for the vendor, invoice number, amount and destination account. The result stays visible for a person to review, and the provider status appears beside the uploaded file. The upload alone does not create an assessment, and extracted identity does not select or verify a vendor by itself.

I check the extracted values, select the known synthetic vendor record and confirm the destination. The reviewer can edit candidate fields before submitting. A mismatch remains visible even after the fields are confirmed. Here, the requested bank account differs from the saved vendor account. ScamShield keeps those two values visible so the reviewer can see the discrepancy before deciding what to do.

After submission, the policy returns Review. The account change is one recorded signal. Gemini classifies the request message, which asks the recipient to bypass a callback. The policy turns validated evidence codes into a fixed explanation and recommends pausing the simulated payment while the reviewer checks through a trusted, independent contact. The displayed summary uses a deterministic template; Gemini does not write it. This is evidence for human review, not a finding that fraud has occurred.

A second synthetic invoice uses the saved vendor destination and returns Continue because no configured hold was met. Continue means no configured rule requested intervention; it is not an approval or a safety guarantee. Case history retains both the routine invoice and the changed-account review, so a reviewer can compare their outcomes. The case report can be downloaded. The Firestore records remained available after a Cloud Run revision change.

The app runs on Cloud Run, uses Firebase anonymous authentication and Firestore, and calls Vertex AI Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite. Tests confirmed one visitor cannot read another visitor’s cases. The runtime limits model attempts to nine per visitor per day, fifty globally per day and two hundred total. A one-hundred-rupee budget alert runs through November sixth; alerts do not stop charges. Fifty-nine backend tests passed, but test counts do not measure fraud accuracy. This is a human-reviewed prototype using synthetic data, not a payment system.
